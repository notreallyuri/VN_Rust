# Scenes and characters

A script is made of scenes, and each scene is a linear sequence of instructions. Scene
names have to be unique, they are what `jump` targets, and execution starts at whichever
scene the game names as its entry.

```story
scene archive_start:
  background archive_office with fade
  music archive
  show registrar neutral at right with dissolve
  registrar "You're the new one. Sit."
  "The lamps are never put out down here."
```

## Text mode

A scene is `adv` by default: one line at a time in the dialogue box. `nvl` reads it as
full-screen pages instead, each line printed below the last.

```story
scene ruins nvl:
  "The corridor narrows."
  mary "I have been here before."
  "Her voice does not echo."
```

`adv` and `nvl` are the only modes. The mode belongs to the scene, so a jump changes it,
and a save or a rollback restores it without storing anything of its own. The page is
built from the lines the player has read, which is why it survives a load and steps back
with rollback; it starts empty and turns over when it is full.

## Showing a character

```story
show mary tired at left
show hugo neutral at right
mary "Father."
show mary angry
```

Both the character and the image have to exist in the engine's registry. Re-showing a
character updates the image, and re-showing without `at` keeps the spot — so changing an
expression never moves anyone. Characters shown without a position are spread evenly.

Positions are `far_left`, `left`, `center`, `right` and `far_right`. A character stands
where you put them until they are removed; `remove` and `clear` forget the spot.

```story
remove hugo
clear
```

`remove` takes one character off the stage. `clear` takes all of them, and leaves the
background alone.

## Backgrounds

```story
background study_night
background none
```

The backdrop is replaced whole, and stays until the next `background` — across jumps and
across scenes. Characters on screen are not affected. Where the image lives is the
engine's business: for the default engine, `assets/backgrounds/<image_id>.png`.

## Transitions

```story
show moriarty older at right with dissolve
show moriarty wary at left with dissolve 0.8
remove moriarty with slide_left
background santa_ilde_courtyard with fade
clear with shake
```

| Transition | Characters | Backgrounds | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dissolve` | fade in, fade out, crossfade between expressions | crossfade | 0.5 s |
| `fade` | like `dissolve` | through black | 1 s |
| `slide_left` | enter from the right edge, leave to the left | the new one pushes the old out to the left | 0.6 s |
| `slide_right` | enter from the left edge, leave to the right | pushed out to the right | 0.6 s |
| `shake` | instant, and the screen shakes | the same | 0.4 s |
| `flash` | instant, and the screen flashes | the same | 0.3 s |

`with` goes last on the line, and the length is in seconds, above 0 and up to 30. Showing
a character who is already on screen with a new expression crossfades the two; give it a
new `at` as well and they move there too. Without `with`, the change is instant.

Transitions do not pause the story: the next line starts while they play, and a click
finishes them. Rollback and loading a save show the end result at once.

`shake` and `flash` are effects rather than motions — whatever the line changes happens at
once, and the whole screen shakes or flashes for that long. `clear with shake` and
`background storm with flash` are the usual ways to punctuate a moment.

## Music and sound

```story
music santa_ilde
music none
sound door_open
voice registrar_01
```

`music` starts a looping track and replaces whatever was playing; the default engine
crossfades, and `music none` fades out. Like the background, music carries across jumps
and scenes, is saved, and rolls back with the story — and naming the track that is already
playing does nothing.

`sound` is a one-shot effect: not saved, not replayed on rollback or load. `voice` plays a
clip for the next line of dialogue and stops when the player moves on; auto mode waits for
it.

The engine decides where the files live. For the default engine that is
`assets/music/`, `assets/sounds/` and `assets/voice/`, as `.ogg`, `.mp3`, `.wav` or
`.flac`. A missing file is a warning, and the game stays silent there.
