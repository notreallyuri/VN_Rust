# Choices and jumps

```story
choice:
  "Agree":
    "You nod silently."
    jump next_scene

  "Disagree":
    "You shake your head."
    jump argument_scene
```

A choice opens a block and needs at least one option. Each option is a non-empty quoted
string, ends with a colon, and contains at least one instruction. Nothing falls through to
the next option and nothing is implied — a quoted line ending in a colon outside a
`choice:` block is an error rather than narration.

## Gating an option

```story
set has_key = true
choice:
  "Open the door" when has_key == true "The door is locked":
    "The lock gives."
  "Look through the window" unless curtains == true:
    "You see a table, and nothing else."
  "Turn back":
    "You leave the way you came."
```

`when` offers the option only while the condition holds, and `unless` is its opposite. The
conditions are the ones `if` takes, variables included.

Without a reason, an option whose condition fails is not shown at all. With a reason, it is
shown but cannot be taken, and the reason is what the player reads when they point at it.
Reasons are translated like any other line.

`novn check` warns when every option of a choice can be hidden, since a choice with
nothing left to offer is skipped at runtime.

## Pictures on an option

```story
choice:
  "The north road" image north preview north_view:
    "Stones, and then trees."
  "The river path" image river:
    "Water, loud enough to hide a voice."
```

`image` draws `choices/<image_id>.png` on the option itself, and the game decides whether
that fills the button or sits beside the label as an icon. `preview` shows
`previews/<image_id>.png` while the option is under the pointer or has the focus. Both are
ids like any other asset, not paths.

Between an option's text and its colon come these modifiers, each at most once, in any
order. `when`, `unless`, `image` and `preview` mean what they say only there; everywhere
else they are ordinary words.

## Jumping

```story
jump vault
```

`jump` transfers execution to another scene and ends the current one. The target has to
exist, which is one of the things `novn check` verifies before the game runs.
